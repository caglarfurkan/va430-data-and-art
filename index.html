2 · Art dataWhat is art data?

Art data is more than metadata.

Metadata

who made it, when, medium, dimensions, when the museum bought it

Pixels

color palette, brightness, and edges measured from a photo of the work

Text

wall labels, exhibition catalogues, press reviews, artist interviews

Georges Seurat, A Sunday on La Grande Jatte — 1884

People & networks

artists, curators, galleries, collectors, and who exhibits with whom

Market & provenance

who owned the work, auction prices, public or private funding

Audience

how long visitors look, where they stand, or our own classroom survey

It can become:a charta mapa networkan image plotan objecta drawinga sound
Left column: data taken directly from the object or its catalogue record. Right column: how the work moves through people, money, and viewers.

2 · Art dataCleaning

Metadata is an interpretation.

Real “Date” values from 3,000 MoMA records → a rule that keeps the first 4-digit year

  • (c. 1915)→1915circa dropped
  • early 1930s→1930a decade, not a single year
  • 1884-86→1884range, hyphen
  • 1962–66→1962range, en dash
  • 1953—1956→1953range, em dash
  • 1971 (printed 2014)→1971negative vs. print date
  • Published 1926 (frottage 1925)→1926rule picks publication year
  • Before 1899→1899only an upper limit
  • n.d.→—no date, dropped
633of 3,000 dates are not a plain year
3different dash characters
55listed as “Unidentified” or “Unknown”

Keep the raw column.

Never overwrite what the museum wrote.

Write down your rule.

“First year in the text” is a choice you made; explain it.

Count what you drop.

The rows you leave out are part of the result.

3 · Live exerciseContext

Now, the context.

  • who made it, and howTwo years of studio work after 60+ sketches; Seurat placed pure color dots side by side to mix in the viewer’s eye, then added a painted dot border in 1888–89.
  • when, where, for whomAn island on the Seine outside Paris: bourgeois Sunday strollers share the bank with working-class rowers; the pet monkey on a leash was a well-known Parisian satire trope.
  • posture & stillnessAlmost every figure stands in strict profile or frontal pose like an ancient frieze—crowded together, yet nobody speaks to or looks at anyone else.
  • what happened to the paintSeurat’s unstable zinc yellow dots darkened to brown within a few years, shifting the warmth and light balance we see today.
Once we talk through the social tension, the frozen frieze-like postures, and the darkened yellow pigment, let's see if anyone's rating of calm/tense or ordered/chaotic changes.
